Peak Lead Exposure Score (0 – 100)

The Peak Exposure Score is a real-time, physics-based index (calibrated from 0 to 100) that quantifies aerosolized tetraethyllead exposure risk at your monitored property for each aircraft flyover.

1. Exposure Risk Spectrum

Score Range Severity Level Aviation & Dispersion Context
0 / 100 Clean / None Unleaded turbine, turboprop (Jet-A), commercial airline, electric, or zero flights. Zero airborne lead.
1 – 39 / 100 Low / Moderate 100LL piston aircraft passing at higher cruise altitudes (>1,500 ft AGL) or upwind. Plume drifts away from property; minimal ground deposition.
40 – 69 / 100 Elevated Downwind 100LL aircraft passing within 1.5 NM directly upwind, or within 1,200 ft slant range. Detectable ground-level plume concentration (0.5 – 2.0 μg/m³).
70 – 100 / 100 Critical / Direct Plume Direct low-altitude takeoff or climb-out (high engine power factor) passing directly overhead and upwind. Highest aerosolized lead concentration (2.0 – 5.0+ μg/m³).

2. Mathematical & Physical Formulation

The score synthesizes two coupled atmospheric and aerodynamic phenomena:

Score = min(100, (C_ground × 25.0) + (Overhead_Factor × 40.0 × Wind_Alignment))
  • Aerosolized Lead Emission Rate (Q): Reciprocating piston aircraft burning 100LL AvGas contain 2.12 grams of lead per gallon. Real-time fuel burn rate is modulated by engine power setting: full-throttle climb-out (100% power factor) vs. descent/approach (45% power factor).
  • Gaussian Plume Dispersion (C_ground): Uses standard Pasquill-Gifford atmospheric diffusion equations:
    C(x, y, z) = [ Q / (π · u · σ_y · σ_z) ] · exp(-y² / 2σ_y²) · exp(-H² / 2σ_z²)
    Where u is effective wind speed from your weather station, y is lateral crosswind displacement, H is flight altitude AGL, and σ_y, σ_z are atmospheric dispersion coefficients.
  • Wind Vector Alignment: Calculates whether the house falls inside the downwind lateral exhaust cone (±35° from wind transport axis).
  • Wingtip Vortex Downwash (Overhead_Factor): For close passes (<1,500 ft slant range), aircraft wingtip vortices force engine exhaust downward into the immediate ground boundary layer regardless of regional wind direction.

3. EPA Regulatory & Health Context

Under the Clean Air Act, the EPA National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) for lead is 0.15 μg/m³ (3-month rolling average). While high-altitude aircraft disperse lead over wide areas, low-altitude general aviation flyovers produce localized ground-level bursts of 0.5 to 5.0+ μg/m³, which settle into soil and household dust. ALEMS logs every event with cryptographic SHA-256 hashes to provide verifiable, audit-ready corroboration for regulatory and community advocacy.
